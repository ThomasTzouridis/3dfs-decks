What we saw at Emulate

"The future of drug development is human." Emulate builds organ-chips, microphysiological systems that replicate human organ function for drug testing and development.

The Liver-Chip recreates the human liver sinusoid using a two-channel microfluidic device with four cell types: primary human hepatocytes, liver sinusoidal endothelial cells, Kupffer cells, and stellate cells. The system maintains vascular flow to simulate realistic liver response.

The site presents this technology through 3D renderings of the chip, microscopy images of the cells, and clinical validation data. Emulate emphasizes their FDA distinction and partnership opportunities with pharmaceutical companies.

Idea 1: the Liver-Chip explained

One option: a short, scientifically accurate 3D animation of how the Emulate Liver-Chip works. A rough sketch of the structure:

1. Architecture

A two-channel microfluidic device with endothelial cells (vascular layer), primary human hepatocytes (middle chamber), and supporting cells (Kupffer and stellate cells).

2. Perfusion

Continuous microfluidic flow simulates physiological shear stress and nutrient delivery, maintaining realistic cell behavior and drug response.

3. Response

When a drug is added, the Liver-Chip measures real-time toxicity and cellular response, replacing months of animal and laboratory testing.

Idea 3: drug screening accuracy

An example of a topic that is easier to show than to describe.

Traditional animal models predict drug-induced liver injury (DILI) with approximately 30% accuracy. Spheroids and conventional cultures reach about 57%. The Liver-Chip achieves 87% accuracy with 100% specificity, accepted into the FDA ISTAND program.

This comparison is the centerpiece of Emulate's regulatory and commercial case. A visual side-by-side makes the advantage immediate and memorable.

This chart could appear in investor decks, regulatory submissions, pharmaceutical sales presentations, and congress posters, always with the same data and visual language.
