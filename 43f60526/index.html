Idea 1: how Immunopheresis works

One option: a short, scientifically accurate animation of the three-step process. A rough sketch of the structure:

1. Collection

Blood is drawn and passes through a cartridge containing antibody-coated nanobeads.

2. Binding

The nanobeads capture molecules that suppress immune response, filtering them from the blood.

3. Return

Filtered blood returns to the body with immune barriers removed, enabling T-cells to attack cancer.

An animation like this is usually built once and then adapted for different audiences. The next slide shows what that could look like.

Idea 3: immune activation over treatment

An example of a topic that is easier to show than to describe.

A patient's T-cell activity before, during, and after Immunopheresis treatment follows a clear narrative arc. Baseline shows suppressed immune activity; treatment gradually restores it; sustained follow-up shows lasting effect.

Explaining why immune activation matters for cancer outcomes is a visual story that works better as animation than text. Today it is explained in written clinical trial results.

This type of graphic could appear on the clinical trials page, in patient materials, in conference presentations and in partnerships discussions with hospitals and pharma companies.
